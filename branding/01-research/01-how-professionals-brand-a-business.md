# How professionals brand a business — and how it was applied to Menu Master

Branding is not a logo. A logo is the last ten percent. The first ninety is
deciding who the business is for, what it stands for, and how it should feel,
so that every later decision (a colour, a word, a screen) can be tested
against something fixed.

## The standard process (agency practice)

The process below is the common shape across agency guides such as
[ewm.swiss](https://ewm.swiss/en/blog/guide-designing-brand-identity),
[merge.rocks](https://merge.rocks/resources/websites-playbook/brand-strategy-positioning/what-are-the-stages-of-the-branding-process-5-game-changing-steps-every-founder-must-know),
[Centric DXB's guideline process](https://www.centricdxb.com/insights/how-to-create-brand-guidelines),
[ManyPixels](https://www.manypixels.co/blog/post/how-to-create-branding-guidelines) and
Michael Johnson's book *Branding in Five and a Half Steps*
([summary PDF](https://advocacy.ccrjustice.org/Resources/cW4ZYv/1AD043/BrandingInFiveAndAHalfSteps.pdf)).
None is an official standard; together they are what a good studio does.

| Step | What the professional does | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Discovery and audit | Interviews the founder and customers. Reads what exists. Lists competitors. Finds what the product truly does that others do not. | Research notes, competitor map, audit of current assets |
| 2. Strategy | Writes purpose, positioning, audience, values, personality. The one sentence that would be true even if the logo changed. | Brand strategy document |
| 3. Verbal identity | Name check, tagline, key messages, voice and tone with do/don't examples. | Messaging and voice guide |
| 4. Visual identity | Logo system, colour, type, imagery, graphic devices. Many concepts, then one. Tested small, in black and white, on a phone, on a shop sign. | Logo files, palette, type, imagery direction |
| 5. System and guidelines | Rules so other people can use it without the designer in the room. Templates for the things the business makes every week. | Brand guidelines, templates |
| 5½. Rollout and governance | Launch across every touchpoint at once. Keep one owner of the guide. Review yearly. | Launch checklist, owner |

Three habits separate professional work from decoration:

1. **Strategy before pixels.** No colour is picked until the audience and positioning are written.
2. **One idea per logo.** A mark says one thing. Two ideas in one mark say nothing.
3. **Design for the worst case.** The logo on a 16 px favicon, a faded flyer, a WhatsApp display picture, a receipt printed in one colour. If it works there, it works anywhere.

## How it was applied here

| Step | Done for Menu Master |
|---|---|
| Discovery | Read `README.md`, `STATUS.md`, the landing page copy, the plan features, the three usability personas (buka owner, caterer, baker) and every app route. Audited the current identity: an "MM" tile, system fonts, a green accent (`#1F7A52`) already live and working in the product. |
| Strategy | `02-brand-strategy/brand-strategy.md`. Positioning: the money-truth app for Nigerian food businesses. Audience: owners who sell food and do their accounts in their head. |
| Verbal | Tagline kept from the product ("Know your cost. Know your price. Know your profit."). Voice guide with examples drawn from the real UI copy. |
| Visual | The owner's logo (the Rising Bowl) rebuilt as vector artwork with a full variant set. Palette taken from the supplied logo: forest green and harvest gold, with the live UI green kept for buttons. Free Google Fonts. Real Nigerian food and market photography, verified links. |
| System | Guidelines page, tokens, templates for the things the business posts and prints. |
| Rollout | `02-brand-strategy/rollout-checklist.md`. |

## Competitive frame (for positioning only)

Menu Master's nearest comparisons are not other costing apps; the owner's
real alternatives are a notebook, a WhatsApp group, or "the shop next door
charges ₦2,500 so I do too". Globally, restaurant costing tools (MarketMan,
Apicbase, xtraCHEF) are built for multi-site restaurants with a laptop in the
office. Nigerian fintech brands (bank and POS apps) own the words *money* and
*safe*. Menu Master's open ground is **"what this plate really costs you"**,
said in plain language, on a phone, from the owner's own receipts. The brand
therefore borrows trust cues from fintech (green, clean numbers, calm layout)
and warmth from food (harvest gold, real pots, real markets), and never
looks like a spreadsheet.
