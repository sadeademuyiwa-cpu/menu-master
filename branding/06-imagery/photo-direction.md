# Photo direction

Menu Master's pictures show **the owner's world, as it is**: pots, plates,
markets, hands, phones. Not stock "business people", not a laptop on a
marble desk, not a chef in a tall white hat.

## Choose photos that are

- **Real Nigerian food, cooked for sale.** A 50-litre pot, not a styled single plate on linen. Jollof, egusi, amala, small chops, meat pies, cakes.
- **Natural light, warm tones.** Palm-oil orange, tomato red, rice gold, green leaves. These are the brand colours, which is why the palette works.
- **Mid-action.** Stirring, serving, counting money, weighing, loading a crate, holding a phone. Something is being done.
- **Honest.** Plastic chairs, aluminium pots, market umbrellas, a danfo in the background. The brand is proud of the buka.
- **Focused on hands and food**, with faces in the background or in profile. Faces are fine where the person is clearly comfortable (smiling vendors); never use a face to imply "she uses Menu Master".

## Avoid

- Dark, moody restaurant photography.
- Filters that push green or blue; the warmth is the point.
- Western fast food, sushi, burgers.
- Generic "African" visuals: safari, kente patterns as decoration, drums.
- Photos with visible competitor logos, bank logos, or a POS brand.
- Anything with a face used as a testimonial without a signed release.

## Treatment

- Crop tight. Phone screens are small; the subject should fill a third of the frame.
- Pair every photo with a number or a sentence in the brand type. A photo alone is not a Menu Master post.
- Overlay colour: Deep Green at 70–85 % opacity, with white text, or a white card on top of the photo. Never a black gradient.
- Corner radius 16 px on cards, 24 px on social tiles, 0 on full-bleed.

## Shooting your own (cheapest, best)

A phone in daylight at a customer's buka beats stock. Shoot:
1. The pot at the moment the lid comes off.
2. The plate being dished.
3. The price board.
4. The market receipt or the owner's notebook.
5. The owner's hand holding her phone with the dish page open (consent first, ₦5,000 and a free month is fair).
